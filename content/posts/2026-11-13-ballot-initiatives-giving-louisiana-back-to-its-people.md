A ballot initiative is a form of direct democracy that allows citizens to propose legislation or constitutional changes and submit those proposals to voters after collecting a legally required number of petition signatures. A ballot initiative is somewhat similar to how we can recall elected officials by collecting enough signatures within a specified timespan. Unlike ordinary legislation introduced by lawmakers and enacted by elected representatives, ballot initiatives allow voters to begin the lawmaking process themselves (University of Wisconsin Law School, 2023). Ballot initiatives in the state of Louisiana would allow everyday people to bypass legislative inaction, take political power into our own hands, and expand our individual rights.

Louisiana is not necessarily known for being fast. After all, our largest city is dubbed "The Big Easy." The same seems to be true for our incompetent legislature. A study by researchers at Princeton University and Northwestern University found that average citizens had little or no independent influence on U.S. policymaking when the preferences of economic elites and organized interest groups were taken into account (Gilens & Page, 2014). This isn't just the opinion of some fancy researchers. This sentiment can be felt in the Bayou State, too.

For example, in April 2022, researchers at the University of New Orleans (UNO) conducted a survey that reported approximately 58% of registered voters supported the legalization of recreational marijuana (Chervenak & Licciardi, 2022). Yet, our lawmakers remain stagnant. Where we saw gains through expansion of Louisiana's medical marijuana program under former Governor John Bel Edwards, we have yet to see recreational marijuana legalized under Jeff Landry.

In February 2026, Democratic Representative Candace Newell introduced a bill that would establish a temporary pilot program for recreational marijuana in Louisiana for adults 21 and older. The bill was referred to the House Committee on Health and Welfare in February and was killed by June (La. H.B. 373, 2026).

If Louisiana had a statewide ballot initiative program, citizens could bypass partisan legislative gridlock and vote directly on laws that Louisianans actually want. Had Louisiana lawmakers fought for our right to voice our opinions directly, we might already be collecting tax revenue from legalized recreational marijuana, as other states like Missouri, Colorado, and New York do. Colorado alone collected $231.1 million in marijuana-related revenue during its 2024–2025 fiscal year, with portions of marijuana tax revenue supporting public education (Colorado Legislative Council Staff, 2026). Similar revenue in Louisiana could support local schools, teachers, and everyday working people.

Louisiana residents deserve to have their voices heard and their wishes respected instead of ignored.

While some parishes, like Lafayette and Livingston Parish, have local ballot initiative processes, these rights should be expanded across the state and to the state level for all Louisiana citizens. Establishing statewide ballot initiatives would allow citizens to transform public demands into enforceable legal protections.

If other states have given their citizens this power, why are Louisianans still forced to wait for permission from the Legislature?

In 2018, Florida citizens used an initiative petition to place Amendment 4 before voters. This amendment was approved by 64.55% of voters and provided for the restoration of voting rights for most people with felony convictions who had completed their sentences, excluding those convicted of murder or felony sexual offenses. Subsequent legislation imposed additional requirements affecting its implementation (Ballotpedia, n.d.; Florida Senate, 2019).

More recently, in 2022, Michigan organizers submitted 753,759 signatures to place a constitutional amendment protecting reproductive freedom, including abortion rights, before voters. Proposal 3 was approved with 56.7% of the vote (Michigan Department of Attorney General, 2024).

Normal people came together after identifying an unfulfilled need and created change on their own, without waiting for lawmakers pressured by powerful sheriff associations*. Louisianans deserve this right, too.

I believe in a Louisiana that is led for the People and by the People, not just in idiom, but in reality. I see Louisiana using ballot initiatives to support popular programs like Medicaid, establish state-level healthcare that isn't tied to employment, support marijuana legislation, and expel surveillance cameras and data centers from our neighborhoods. States like Oregon and Washington have already taken steps toward expanding healthcare coverage outside employer-sponsored insurance through programs like Oregon's OHP Bridge and Washington's Cascade Select public option (Oregon Health Authority, n.d.; Washington State Health Care Authority, n.d.).

I see Louisianans working together and relying on one another instead of waiting for change that is never going to come, or that won't arrive until the afterlife.

Louisiana consistently struggles with serious problems involving poverty, education, healthcare, and quality of life. Yet, I believe our politicians are more motivated by financial and political gain than by working for the plight of the people.

But a vision alone will not change our laws. So what would it take for ballot initiatives to become a reality here at home, not in Florida or Michigan?

Louisiana residents have a direct constitutional pathway for creating statewide citizen ballot initiatives: passing an amendment through the Legislature.

## What Would It Take?

Under Article XIII of the Louisiana Constitution, a constitutional amendment requires 70 out of 105 House votes and 26 out of 39 Senate votes. Then, more than 50% of voters voting on the amendment would have to approve it for adoption (La. Const. art. XIII, § 1).

There are a few steps involved in making this happen:

1. Draft a proposed constitutional amendment establishing citizen initiative powers.
2. Secure a Louisiana representative or senator to sponsor it as a joint resolution.
3. Obtain legislative committee consideration and a public hearing.
4. Secure two-thirds approval in both legislative chambers.
5. Submit the amendment to voters in a statewide election.
6. Win approval from more than 50% of voters voting on the amendment.

## So What Can You Do?

There's no need to co-sponsor a bill or get in your Sunday best for a meeting. You can start by voicing your support for statewide Louisiana ballot initiatives through an email or phone call.

Email and call your representatives. You can find out who they are and locate their contact information through the Louisiana Secretary of State's voter resources and the Legislature's website.

Here's a general email you can send to your representatives:

---

**Subject: Request for Legislation Establishing Statewide Citizen Initiative Powers in Louisiana**

Dear Representative [Last Name],

I am writing as a Louisiana constituent to urge you to introduce and support legislation proposing a constitutional amendment that would establish **statewide citizen initiative and referendum powers in Louisiana**.

Currently, Louisiana residents cannot independently place proposed state laws or constitutional amendments on the ballot through citizen petitions. Instead, the power to propose statewide constitutional amendments rests with the Legislature. This limits citizens' ability to directly address issues when their elected representatives are unwilling or unable to act.

I believe Louisianans deserve a greater voice in the laws that govern our lives. Citizens should have the right to propose legislation, gather signatures, and bring qualifying measures before voters without requiring prior legislative approval.

I respectfully ask that you support a constitutional amendment establishing:

1. **Citizen-initiated statutes**, allowing residents to propose state laws through a petition process.
2. **Citizen-initiated constitutional amendments**, allowing voters to propose changes to the Louisiana Constitution.
3. **Popular referendums**, allowing residents to petition for a statewide vote on legislation already enacted.
4. **Fair and accessible petition requirements**, ensuring that ordinary citizens and grassroots organizations can participate without unreasonable financial or administrative barriers.
5. **Transparent and enforceable procedures**, protecting the integrity of the initiative process and preventing undue political interference.

Numerous states already provide their residents with these democratic powers. Louisiana should afford its citizens the same opportunity to participate directly in government.

**I ask that you introduce a constitutional amendment establishing statewide citizen initiative and referendum powers during the next legislative session.**

Please let me know whether you support this reform and whether you would be willing to sponsor or co-sponsor such legislation.

Thank you for your time and service to Louisiana.

Sincerely,

[Your Full Name]  
[City or Parish], Louisiana
